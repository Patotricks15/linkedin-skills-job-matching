\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
The central limitation is the one documented in Section~2: there is no real
candidate-job interaction signal, only a self-supervised proxy built from
each posting's own (partially resampled) requirements. The reported numbers
demonstrate that the pipeline mechanics are sound and leakage-free, not that
the system would satisfy a real candidate's job search. Both BM25 and
LambdaMART are also purely lexical and statistical: neither captures
semantic skill similarity outside the hand-built taxonomy (a candidate
strong in PyTorch but not listing "machine learning" verbatim gets no
credit a learned embedding space would have given them), which is precisely
the capability a neural dense retriever would add on top of this
foundation. The project's contribution is therefore not a claim that
lexical retrieval plus boosted-tree re-ranking is sufficient for production
job matching, but a demonstration of the classical, production-proven
predecessor to neural dense retrieval: a leakage-resistant, fully tested
two-stage pipeline where every feature is auditable, every split is
point-in-time safe, and the one lever available in place of a learned
embedding space -- taxonomy normalization -- is shown, quantitatively, to
matter.
